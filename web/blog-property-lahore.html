How to Buy Property in Lahore Safely

Home  /  Blog  /  Property Lahore Checklist
Buyer's Checklist May 5, 2026 7 min read

Buying property in Lahore is a milestone — but with cash deals, file trading, and unregistered dealers, it's also where many buyers lose money. This is the exact 7-step checklist our agents run before any client signs. Follow it and you'll dodge the vast majority of property fraud cases in Lahore.

Step 1 — Verify the Society & NOC

Before anything else, confirm the society itself is legally approved. Every legitimate Lahore society publishes its NOC (No Objection Certificate) from LDA, RUDA or the relevant cantonment authority. Ask the dealer for a copy and cross-check the NOC number on the LDA / RUDA website. If a property is in a non-approved scheme, you can lose the entire payment when the scheme is sealed.

Step 2 — Check the Property's Title Documents

For any property in Lahore, the seller should be able to produce:

  • Original allotment / transfer letter issued by the society.
  • Membership form & payment receipts — the full chain from first allottee to current owner.
  • Possession letter (for plots) or completion certificate (for built houses).
  • Latest utility bills in the seller's name, or "no dues" certificate from the society.

Never accept "the file is at the lawyer's office" as an answer. Originals first, payment after.

Step 3 — Confirm the Seller's Identity

Match the seller's CNIC to the name on the transfer letter. If the seller is an overseas Pakistani or selling through a Power of Attorney, verify the POA is registered with NADRA and the Pakistani consulate. For inheritance cases, ask for the legal heirs certificate and ensure every heir has signed.

Step 4 — Property Inspection in Person

Visit the actual plot or house. Walk the boundary, verify the plot number on the corner pillar, and check the road width against the society map. If you're buying a built house, hire an independent contractor for a 1-hour structural inspection — it usually costs Rs 10,000 and can save you crores.

Step 5 — Get an Independent Dealer Verification

Even if you trust your seller, get a second dealer in the same society to independently confirm the current market price and recent transactions for the same block. Property in Lahore can be inflated by 15–25% by unscrupulous dealers — a second opinion takes one phone call.

Step 6 — Payment & Transfer the Safe Way

The transfer itself should happen at the society office, in front of the transfer officer. Best practice:

  • Pay 1–3% token via pay-order or bank transfer (never cash) after document verification.
  • Sign a written agreement specifying date of full payment and transfer.
  • Pay the balance on transfer day, at the society office, with the transfer letter issued before you hand over funds.
  • Keep stamped receipts of every rupee.

Step 7 — Red Flags to Walk Away From

  • Price 20%+ below market "because the owner is in urgent need."
  • Pressure to pay token within 24 hours.
  • Seller insists on cash; refuses bank transfer.
  • Original documents "always somewhere else."
  • The dealer won't put their name and CNIC on a written agreement.
